Core Principles & Thesis
01

The Degree Was a Proxy. The Proxy Broke.

A university degree never measured what you could actually do. It measured your willingness to endure a four-year filter. For decades, employers used it as an easy sorting mechanism because measuring true capability was too costly.

In the AI era, that condition has completely collapsed. AI models and modern evaluation tools can inspect and verify real work product in seconds. When you can verify real capability cheaply, paying $150,000 for a broken proxy no longer makes sense.

"Proxies survive only while measuring the real thing stays expensive. That era is over."
02

Proof of Understanding Over Passive Credentials

AI makes it trivially easy to generate superficial code, essays, or pitch decks. Anyone can prompt a model and claim to know the answer. The real risk in the modern workplace isn't just fooling recruiters — it's fooling yourself.

Orbix replaces passive credentials with Proof of Understanding. You build real production software, design real agents, orchestrate complex systems, and defend your architectural choices. When recruiters review your Orbix profile, they don't see grades — they see immutable evidence of problem-solving.

"Instead of a certificate claiming you're capable, you build an inspectable record of real problems solved."
03

Aligned Incentives: We Charge Recruiters, Not Students

Universities make money upfront regardless of whether their graduates find a job. Bootcamps lock students into aggressive debt and Income Share Agreements. Both models put the financial risk on the learner.

Orbix flips the business model entirely. We never charge students tuition. We partner directly with leading technology companies, startups, and top recruiters who compete to hire verified talent. When you get hired, recruiters pay us. You win, we win.

"If our graduates don't land high-impact careers, we don't get paid. That is how education should always work."
04

Why We Refuse to Be an "EdTech" Company

EdTech's premise was that traditional schooling was correct and merely suffered from poor digital distribution. So they spent 20 years recording lectures on video and gamifying multiple-choice quizzes.

Orbix is not EdTech. We are building Human Infrastructure for the AI era. Just as the industrial age required power grids and railroads, the intelligence age requires infrastructure that compounds human potential and agency.

"The world is short humans. We're long."
